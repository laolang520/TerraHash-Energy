BITCOIN MINING · EXPLAINED

What is a Bitcoin miner?

A Bitcoin miner is a specialized computing machine, typically built around application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) chips, designed to perform SHA-256 calculations for the Bitcoin network. Miners repeatedly process cryptographic computations as part of Bitcoin's proof-of-work system, contributing computing power — or hashrate — to network security and transaction settlement.

At industrial scale, a miner is only one component of a larger operating system. Reliable mining requires electrical infrastructure, transformers and distribution, network connectivity, cooling or thermal management, monitoring, maintenance and disciplined fleet operations.

Energy is the input. Hashrate is the output.
01 / HARDWAREASIC Computing

Purpose-built hardware optimized for BTC SHA-256 computation rather than general-purpose computing.

02 / POWERContinuous Energy Demand

Large fleets require reliable, scalable electricity and site-level power systems designed for dense loads.

03 / THERMALCooling & Operations

Airflow, liquid or immersion cooling, maintenance and uptime management influence operating efficiency.

04 / OUTPUTHashrate

Electrical energy supports specialized computation that contributes hashrate to the Bitcoin network.
