# TerraHash Energy — Website Consistency & Evidence Review
Date: 2026-11-06
Status: Editorial / legal / operational review required — not an audit opinion

## Scope
Repository-wide HTML inventory: 70 HTML files (public pages, historical year pages and design previews), plus site data, scripts, navigation and governance download references. This document identifies public-facing statements that could be misunderstood. It does not determine whether historical transactions occurred.

## Priority 1 — Historical claims and legal-entity attribution
- The U.S. company is described as an early-stage, independently managed California LLC. The 2013–2026 international history elsewhere describes completed investments, funding rounds, deployed sites and managed mining capacity. These accounts must not be conflated.
- 2022 materials refer to a $150 million Series C, named investors, a large power agreement and 2.8 EH/s. Require executed financing evidence, named-party confirmation and operating records.
- 2023 materials mention a CATL battery arrangement, HK$1 billion capital program, claimed electricity costs and 4.5 EH/s. Require supplier confirmation, project records, funding documents and technical calculations.
- 2024 materials describe a $200 million infrastructure fund, commissioned high-voltage assets and 8.2 EH/s. Require ownership, commissioning and funding evidence.
- 2025 materials describe a $300 million digital-asset treasury fund, on-chain assets, derivatives and 12.5 EH/s. Require custody statements, fund structures, accounting policies and regulatory analysis.
- 2026 materials describe a $500 million power fund, 16.8 EH/s, an office/steering entity and Southwest solar-storage projects. Require current contracts, legal status, site rights, grid applications and independently substantiated performance.
- The early-year pages (2013 onward) sometimes describe TerraHash Energy itself as the operator. Confirm the exact historical legal entities, brand-use rights, successor relationship and ownership chain. A common brand alone does not establish legal continuity.

## Priority 2 — Presentation safeguards applied in this review
- Removed unverified quantitative claims from prominent performance cards and executive-candidate summaries; retained links to the source archive for document-level review.
- Reframed the investor overview, home page and milestone overview to distinguish historical claims from present-day U.S. operations.
- Archived annual pages should display an immediate documentary-review notice and avoid search indexing pending substantiation.
- The site's data/projects.json contained sample projects labelled Operating/Construction. These illustrative records were removed from public structured data pending verification.
- Fixed two missing local images on contact.html.

## Priority 3 — Further verification before final publication
1. Obtain a current California Secretary of State record and filing images for the LLC; distinguish original formation from any later amendment. California state listings do not independently establish private ownership arrangements.
2. Obtain certified organization charts, shareholder register/operating agreement or equivalent records showing Hong Kong/Cayman relationships and which entity controls each asset.
3. Confirm formal board composition, independent-director status, committee appointments and the precise issuer/entity to which each PDF governance document applies.
4. Verify every executive and board biography against current consented resumes/appointments; check name transliterations, full legal names, roles, and use of photographs. Some public profiles use first names only.
5. For each financing, partnership, power-purchase agreement, mining site and computing metric, collect source documents, entity attribution, dates, third-party permissions and an approval owner. Avoid claims based only on internally authored website prose.
6. Classify website statements as **Operating**, **Contracted**, **Under development**, **Proposed** or **Industry background**; state date and responsible legal entity.
7. Confirm contact-form routing to FormSubmit, its activation, data processing disclosures and recipient verification; do not infer reliable delivery merely from the presence of the form.
8. Review California recruitment/pay-transparency obligations based on actual employee count and posting scope. Verify RSU plan, capitalization, liquidity statements and written candidate offers privately.
9. Review business/stock images for accurate captions and third-party rights; illustrative imagery must not be presented as company-owned projects.
10. Keep concept-stage STERRA and tokenized/settlement ideas separate from licensed financial services or products; validate regulatory language with counsel.
11. Remove obsolete public previews from navigation/sitemap, and review their search indexing; check 2014 page source comments for Chinese developer notes even though rendered copy is English.
12. Reconcile privacy-policy.html with privacy.html and applicant notices, including current vendors, retention and privacy-rights contacts.

## Evidence standard
For every material number or named collaboration, record: statement, source URL/file, legal entity, counterparty, execution date, current status, responsible approver, review date, and permitted public wording. Until verified, avoid presenting the claim as completed business performance.
